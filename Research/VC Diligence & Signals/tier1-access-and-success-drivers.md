# Tier-1 Access and Startup Success Drivers (and what VentureSauce can see early)

*Research date: 2026-10-10. Scope: Sequoia, a16z, General Catalyst (GC). Builds on `Research/Investor Networks/how-investor-networks-work.md` (deal-flow mechanics, herding, Gompers et al. 2020 survey) and `case-studies-hot-founders.md` (Instinct, Thinking Machines, Mercor). Those topics are not repeated here.*

**Labels:** [FACT] = sourced to primary or quality secondary source. [OPINION] = an investor's stated view or our inference. [UNVERIFIED] = single or low-quality source. "Insufficient data found" = we looked and could not confirm.

---

## Executive summary

1. **Founders pay to get a top VC, and the effect is measurable.** Hsu (JF 2004): offers from high-reputation VCs were about **3x more likely to be accepted**, and those VCs bought equity at a **10–14% discount** ([Hsu 2004](https://repository.upenn.edu/mgmt_papers/29)). Sørensen (JF 2007): experienced VCs' portfolios go public more often, but **sorting (picking better companies) explains about twice as much of that as influence (helping them)** ([Sørensen 2007](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v62y2007i6p2725-2762.html)). [FACT] So much of tier-1 "success" is **access to better deals, not value-add**.
2. **Tier-1 firms get access through owned pipelines.** These are scouts (Sequoia gives scouts up to $100K ([TechCrunch 2019](https://techcrunch.com/2019/06/07/a-peek-inside-sequoia-capitals-low-flying-wide-reaching-scout-program/))), packaged pre-seed programs (Sequoia Arc at about $1M; a16z speedrun at $500K for 10% plus $500K more in the next round), and **company creation** (GC has "hatched 45+" companies, including Livongo and Commure, from an $800M Creation fund). [FACT] They see founders **before a round exists**.
3. **Lower-tier firms lose on four things: allocation, speed, check size and signal.** A seed by a "smart-money" VC lifts the chance of raising a Series A from 35% to 51%. If that VC does not follow on, the chance drops to 27% ([CB Insights via NEA/Vator](https://vator.tv/2010-09-12-the-vc-seed-investor-signaling-problem/); older data, 2008–2013). [FACT, dated] Emerging managers get in through niche focus, and the evidence that they outperform is mixed ([PitchBook](https://files.pitchbook.com/website/files/pdf/PitchBook_Analyst_Note_Beyond_Fund_I.pdf)).
4. **Even tier-1 early hit rates are low.** Since 2020, about **4%** of a16z's (28/635) and Sequoia's (22/580) early-stage bets became unicorns ([Strebulaev via TheFundCFO](https://thefundcfo.substack.com/p/250-1200-unicorns-early-backers-25)) [UNVERIFIED secondary]. Across all VC financings, 65% return under 1x ([Correlation Ventures via Seth Levine](https://sethlevine.com/archives/2014/08/venture-outcomes-are-even-more-skewed-than-you-think.html)). [FACT]
5. **The evidence on success predictors conflicts.** Investors *say* "team first" (Gompers et al., in the earlier doc; Bernstein-Korteweg-Laws 2017 experiment). Kaplan-Sensoy-Strömberg (JF 2009) find the *business* stays stable while management turns over, so "bet on the horse." [FACT] The strongest founder-level predictor is **prior success**: 30% for successful serial founders vs 18% for first-timers ([Gompers et al. 2010](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/economists/kovner/performance_persistence)). The mean age of the fastest-growing founders is **45** ([Azoulay et al.](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24489)). [FACT]
6. **AI rewrote the traction bar.** Stripe reports the top 100 AI companies reached $5M ARR in 24 months vs 37 for SaaS ([TechCrunch 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/27/stripe-ceo-says-ai-startups-are-growing-faster-than-saas-ever-did-and-calling-them-wrappers-misses-the-point)). Bessemer's "Supernovas" average about $40M ARR in year 1 at about 25% gross margin ([BVP State of AI 2025](https://www.bvp.com/the-official-state-of-the-cloud-ai)). Lovable reached $100M ARR in 8 months, Mercor in 11, and Cursor in about 12–14. [FACT, company-reported]
7. **For VentureSauce:** **founder history, team composition, hiring velocity, Form D timing and investor names are publicly observable before announcement.** Revenue, NRR and margins are not. [OPINION] The highest-value check is the one investors weight most and verify least: **team and pedigree claims, plus "who else is in."**

## 1. The tier-1 access mechanism (Sequoia, a16z, GC)

### 1.1 Why founders pick them

| Driver | Evidence | Label |
|---|---|---|
| **Brand as a signal to later investors and recruits** | Hsu (JF 2004) used firms with several competing offers. High-reputation VC offers were ~3x more likely to be accepted, at a 10–14% lower price. He concludes VCs' "extra-financial" value is more distinctive than their money ([UPenn repository](https://repository.upenn.edu/mgmt_papers/29); [Wharton summary](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/what-do-entrepreneurs-pay-for-venture-capital-affiliation/)). Strebulaev's analysis: a top-tier early lead "may mean more than money – it's a signal of validation that helps with hiring and future fundraising" ([TheFundCFO summary](https://thefundcfo.substack.com/p/250-1200-unicorns-early-backers-25)). | FACT (Hsu); OPINION (signal quote) |
| **But is it treatment or selection?** | Sørensen (JF 2007, two-sided matching model): experienced VCs' companies IPO more often. **Sorting is ~2x as important as influence** ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v62y2007i6p2725-2762.html); [Chicago Booth Review](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/how-smart-is-smart-money)). | FACT |
| **a16z platform** | a16z describes a "platform model" in which marketing, talent, legal and policy specialists work alongside the investors. A 2014 HBS case counted 22 investment professionals vs 43 recruiting and marketing specialists ([Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreessen_Horowitz)). Revelio Labs estimates ~1,816 total employees (Dec 2025) ([Revelio](https://www.reveliolabs.com/companies/andreessen-horowitz/employees/)). **A "~500-person platform team" figure: insufficient data found.** | FACT (ratio, secondary); total headcount UNVERIFIED |
| **Sequoia Arc** | An open call for pre-seed and seed founders: $1M initial funding plus a multi-week "Company Design" immersion (workshops on customer, product, GTM and PMF), partner 1:1s and peer boards ([arc.sequoiacap.com](https://arc.sequoiacap.com/); [Superscout](https://superscout.co/program/sequoia-arc)). Sources disagree on length (5 vs 7 weeks) and check size ($500K–$1M). | FACT (existence, $1M per official page); details vary |
| **GC "Customer Value" offering** | GC's **Customer Value Fund (CVF)** funds up to **80% of monthly sales and marketing spend** and is repaid from a capped share of revenue from the financed customer cohorts. If the cohort doesn't grow, GC isn't repaid for that period. It has funded 40+ companies, typically spending $2–20M a month on S&M with $30–750M in revenue ([Axios 2024](https://www.axios.com/2024/06/30/general-catalyst-new-alternative); [GC](https://www.generalcatalyst.com/capital)). **A separate GC "Customer Value team" as a platform function: insufficient data found.** CVF is a financing product. | FACT |
| **Follow-on capacity** | All three firms are multi-stage. Crunchbase: "investors with the largest portfolios were those with early-stage access and the resources to continue backing companies as they scale" ([Crunchbase 2026](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/unicorn-investors-ai-robotics-2026-sequoia-khosla/)). a16z speedrun builds in follow-on: $500K for 10%, plus $500K in the next round within 18 months ([TechCrunch 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/11/how-to-get-into-a16zs-super-competitive-speedrun-startup-accelerator-program/); [Causo](https://hub.causo.ai/how-to-apply-to-a16z-speedrun-2026)). | FACT |

### 1.2 Scout programs

- **Sequoia (launched 2009).** Scouts get **up to $100,000** to invest, subject to "a stringent approval process." More than 230 scout-backed companies raised $6B+ in follow-on financing, excluding Uber. Sam Altman's Stripe check as a scout was later worth ~$25M, per Calacanis. Former scouts include Tony Xu (DoorDash) ([TechCrunch 2019](https://techcrunch.com/2019/06/07/a-peek-inside-sequoia-capitals-low-flying-wide-reaching-scout-program/)). [FACT, single quality source; Calacanis figures are his claims]
- **How Sequoia scouts get carry.** Reported split: **45% of carry to the scout, 5% to a shared scout pool, 50% to Sequoia** ([VCBeast](https://vcbeast.com/sequoia-scout-program-reshaping-deal-flow)). [UNVERIFIED, single aggregator source]
- **a16z scouts.** Aggregators describe an "a16z Scout Fund" with ~$10–25K checks and European scouts sourcing companies such as Mistral and ElevenLabs ([Superscout list](https://superscout.co/list/scout-programs-that-pay-in-carry); [VCBeast](https://vcbeast.com/vc-scout-programs-how-they-work)). [UNVERIFIED; no primary a16z source found] General scout economics: 10–20% of carry on the scout's own deals is typical across programs ([VCBeast](https://vcbeast.com/vc-scout-programs-how-they-work)). [UNVERIFIED]
- **Why it matters.** Scouts are usually portfolio founders, so the firm turns its own portfolio's social graph into pre-market deal flow. As Calacanis put it: "If you're smart, your friends are probably pretty smart, too" ([TechCrunch 2019](https://techcrunch.com/2019/06/07/a-peek-inside-sequoia-capitals-low-flying-wide-reaching-scout-program/)). [OPINION]

## 2. Why lower-tier firms get locked out

| Mechanism | Evidence | Label |
|---|---|---|
| **Allocation** | Leads typically take the majority of a priced round and set terms, and pro rata rights let insiders defend ownership. Programs like speedrun hard-code next-round participation and pro rata ([Causo](https://hub.causo.ai/how-to-apply-to-a16z-speedrun-2026)). Carta: insider-led bridge rounds peaked at 38% of rounds vs a ~26% norm ([Carta](https://carta.com/data/newsletter-3-predictions-for-2024/)). | FACT (Carta); mechanism is standard practice |
| **Speed and pre-empts** | In AI, "VCs are courting top AI startups with preempted rounds and perks" ([WaveUp](https://waveup.com/blog/top-vc-investing-in-ai/)) [UNVERIFIED secondary]. Cursor raised again 4 months after its prior round ([TechCrunch Dec 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/12/19/in-just-4-months-ai-coding-assistant-cursor-raised-another-100m-at-a-2-5b-valuation-led-by-thrive-sources-say)). **Systematic pre-empt share data: insufficient data found.** | Mixed |
| **Check size** | AI mega-rounds of $500M+ were ~58% of AI dollars in 2025 ([Radom, citing industry data](https://www.radom.com/insights/exploring-the-surge-in-investment-u-s-ai-startups-secure-significant-funding-in-2025)). A small fund cannot lead such rounds. | UNVERIFIED secondary |
| **Signaling risk** | 35% of VC-backed seed companies raise an A. With a smart-money VC at seed the rate is **51%**, but if that VC does not follow on it falls to **27%** (seed financings 2008–Jun 2013) ([Vator / CB Insights](https://vator.tv/2010-09-12-the-vc-seed-investor-signaling-problem/); [NEA](https://www.nea.com/blog/signal-or-noise-neas-experience-with-signaling-risk-in-seed-investing)). | FACT, dated |
| **Information asymmetry** | Scouts are portfolio founders investing pre-market ([TechCrunch 2019](https://techcrunch.com/2019/06/07/a-peek-inside-sequoia-capitals-low-flying-wide-reaching-scout-program/)). Arc and speedrun pull founders in pre-round. GC creates companies itself. Sequoia had "an inside position on Y Combinator's unicorns" ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/research/billion-dollar-startup-venture-capital)). | FACT |

### 2.1 Quantifying concentration

- **2026 unicorn cohort** (250 companies through Aug 15, 2026). The top 10 investors by count were Sequoia, Khosla, YC, Lightspeed, Founders Fund, a16z, Bessemer, Lux, GC and BoxGroup. **a16z was the most active Series A lead**, with Khosla, Spark and Sequoia at 6 each. YC and Sequoia had the most seed investments ([Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/unicorn-investors-ai-robotics-2026-sequoia-khosla/)). [FACT]
- **Early leads across 1,200+ post-2015 unicorns:** a16z 39, Sequoia 24, Lightspeed 20, Thrive 10 ([Strebulaev via TheFundCFO](https://thefundcfo.substack.com/p/250-1200-unicorns-early-backers-25)). [UNVERIFIED secondary of a LinkedIn post]
- **Share of unicorn seed and A rounds led by the top 10 firms: insufficient data found.** No source gave a clean percentage. The counts above are the best proxy.
- **Hit rates (early-stage since 2020):** a16z 28/635 (4%), Sequoia 22/580 (4%), Thrive 9%, Firebolt 10%. Early specialists (Benchmark, SV Angel, Greylock) reach 6–12% vs a 0.5–2.5% baseline ([TheFundCFO / Strebulaev](https://thefundcfo.substack.com/p/250-1200-unicorns-early-backers-25)). [UNVERIFIED secondary] Separately, ~330 of 1,200 unicorns have since lost $1B+ status (same source).
- **Base rate:** across 21,000+ financings from 2004 to 2013, 65% returned under 1x and 4% returned 10x+ ([Seth Levine on Correlation Ventures](https://sethlevine.com/archives/2014/08/venture-outcomes-are-even-more-skewed-than-you-think.html)). [FACT]

### 2.2 How emerging managers break in

- **Niche:** first-time funds are more likely to run niche strategies while incumbents raise ever-larger generalist pools ([PitchBook "Beyond Fund I"](https://files.pitchbook.com/website/files/pdf/PitchBook_Analyst_Note_Beyond_Fund_I.pdf)). [FACT]
- **Performance is mixed:** 27% of first-time funds were top quartile vs 20% of non-emerging funds (PitchBook Q1 2021). Later PitchBook work found medians of 11.2% (Funds I–II) vs 13.2% (established) ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bswoawj4ce3e4k2qe7ls/portfolio/up-and-coming-managers-dont-always-outperform)). [FACT, conflicting]
- **Being first:** BoxGroup made the 2026 top-10 unicorn investor list as the only seed specialist, despite much smaller funds ([Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/unicorn-investors-ai-robotics-2026-sequoia-khosla/)). [FACT] Firebolt and Thrive show the highest hit rates in Strebulaev's list. [UNVERIFIED]
- **Value-add as an access lever:** insufficient data found on whether it changes allocation outcomes. [OPINION: it mostly works pre-seed, before tier-1 programs engage]

## 3. What makes startups explode, by stage

### 3.1 Founder traits

| Predictor | Evidence | Label |
|---|---|---|
| **Prior exits** | Successful VC-backed founders have a **30%** success rate in their next venture vs **18%** for first-timers and **20%** for prior failures. The persistence comes from **market-timing skill** ([Gompers, Kovner, Lerner & Scharfstein, JFE 2010](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/economists/kovner/performance_persistence); [HLS Forum](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2010/04/16/performance-persistence-in-entrepreneurship-and-venture-capital)). | FACT |
| **Age and industry experience** | Mean founder age for the top 0.1% fastest-growing new firms is **45.0**. Prior experience in the specific industry strongly predicts success ([Azoulay, Jones, Kim & Miranda, AER: Insights](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24489)). | FACT |
| **Pedigree** | ~1/5 of unicorn founders attended Stanford, 17.5% hold an MBA, and 44% were under 40 at unicorn date. These are descriptive statistics only ([Stanford GSB](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/what-makes-unicorns-special-these-numbers-may-hold-answers)). | FACT (descriptive) |
| **"Spiky" achievement** | Lin: "the person has to have a spike, and that spike has to be in the direction that makes sense for the market" ([Fortune 2024](https://www.fortune.com/2024/10/08/sequoias-alfred-lin-on-the-art-and-math-of-spotting-outliers)). **No academic validation found.** | OPINION |
| **Team vs business** | Kaplan, Sensoy & Strömberg (JF 2009), 50 VC-backed firms from business plan to public company: business lines stay "remarkably stable" while management turnover is substantial, so investors should weight "the horse" more ([USC PDF](https://msbfile03.usc.edu/digitalmeasures/bsensoy/pci/2338575137-1.pdf)). Yet VCs *say* team matters most (Gompers et al. 2020, earlier doc). In a randomized AngelList email experiment, investors responded most to **founding-team information**, not to traction or existing investors ([Bernstein, Korteweg & Laws, JF 2017; Stanford GSB](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/shai-bernstein-what-really-matters-early-stage-investors)). | FACT; the contradiction is real |

### 3.2 Team composition and hiring velocity

- **Hiring too fast is a failure predictor.** Startup Genome (3,200 startups) found that **74%** of high-growth internet startups failed through premature scaling. Prematurely scaling teams were ~3x larger than consistent ones at the same stage, and 93% of them never passed $100K MRR ([GeekWire 2011](https://www.geekwire.com/2011/number-reason-startups-fail-premature-scaling/); [Steve Blank](https://steveblank.com/2011/08)). [FACT, but an older industry study rather than peer-reviewed]
- **Velocity after PMF is a positive signal** for VCs, by practitioner consensus. **Peer-reviewed evidence on post-PMF hiring speed: insufficient data found.** [OPINION]
- Botha: "choosing the right team is a crucible moment because if you choose the right first people and they are A+ people they will bring like people along" (quoted in search summary of [Tim Ferriss #618](https://tim.blog/2022/09/01/roelof-botha-transcript/)). [UNVERIFIED wording; we did not locate it in the indexed transcript]

### 3.3 Market timing and "why now"

- In Gompers et al. 2010, serial founders' edge is **timing**: picking the right industry at the right time. [FACT]
- Andreessen (2007): "The only thing that matters is getting to product/market fit." Also: "In a great market — a market with lots of real potential customers — the market pulls product out of the startup" ([pmarchive via Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product-market_fit); [bokardo](https://bokardo.com/archives/origin-product-market-fit/)). [OPINION; the first sentence is verified in search results. The "market pulls product" sentence is from the same essay but was not re-checked against the original text this session, so treat it as UNVERIFIED wording]

### 3.5 Product and organic pull

## 5. Implications for VentureSauce

### 5.1 Which success predictors are publicly observable before the round is announced

| Predictor | Observable pre-announcement? | Where | VentureSauce check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prior exits / serial founder | **Yes** | Crunchbase, press, SEC filings of prior company, LinkedIn | Verify "previously founded X (acquired by Y)": did the acquisition happen, and was it material? |
| Industry experience and age proxy | **Yes** | LinkedIn tenure, publications | Years in domain vs. deck claim |
| Pedigree (lab, school, ex-OpenAI) | **Yes** | LinkedIn, arXiv/Google Scholar, GitHub | "Ex-OpenAI researcher": role, dates, papers |
| "Spike" achievements | **Partly** | Olympiad lists, papers, GitHub stars, competition results | Verify the specific claimed achievement |
| Team composition and hiring velocity | **Yes** | LinkedIn headcount over time, job posts | Headcount vs deck. Flag both inflation and premature scaling (Startup Genome) |
| Who else is investing | **Partly** | Form D (amount sold, related persons), partner tweets, scout ties | Form D vs claimed raise; check that named investors are real |
| Tier-1 program membership (Arc, speedrun) | **Usually** | Program cohort pages, demo days | Verify "Sequoia-backed" claims; Arc ≠ a Sequoia Series A |
| Revenue / ARR, NRR, Rule of 40 | **No** (mostly) | Only company-reported press; occasionally Stripe or app-store proxies | Label "unverifiable." Compare to stage benchmarks and flag outliers (e.g., claimed Supernova growth with no visible hiring) |
| Organic pull | **Partly** | Web traffic, app rankings, GitHub, community size | Proxy signals only |

### 5.2 Product takeaways [OPINION, to test with users]

1. **The team slide carries the most weight and gets the least checking.** Investors respond most to team information (Bernstein et al.) and say team matters most. Most team facts are public, so **team and pedigree verification is the highest-ROI check** VentureSauce can run.
2. **"Who else is in" is a signal outsiders over-weight.** The 51% vs 27% signaling data show how much the presence of a tier-1 name moves outcomes. Checking that a claimed lead or participant actually exists (Form D related persons, firm announcements) directly targets the herding failure mode in the earlier docs.
3. **The locked-out investor is the natural user.** Lower-tier funds, angels and scouts see deals *after* tier-1 firms have shaped them, under time pressure, with no platform team. A cheap first pass matters most to them.
4. **Use AI-era benchmarks rather than SaaS ones, and flag ARR semantics.** A claimed $10M "ARR" at month 6 is plausible in 2026 (Lovable, Mercor), but run-rate vs contracted ARR needs a label.

### 5.3 Questions this raises for user interviews

- When a deck says "backed by [tier-1 firm]," do you check whether that means a lead, a scout check or a program?
- How much does a pedigree line (ex-OpenAI, Stanford PhD) move your decision, and have you ever verified one?
- Would you trust a "headcount trend vs claimed stage" flag?
