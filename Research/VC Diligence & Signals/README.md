## Key takeaways
1. **Most decks die unread.** VCs consider about 101 opportunities per closed deal, and about 72% are rejected on the deck alone, at roughly 3–4 minutes per deck. Full diligence takes about 118 hours and 10 reference calls, and is skipped entirely on pre-emptive term sheets. (Gompers et al., NBER w22587)
2. **VentureSauce's slot** is before the first meeting and before a pre-emptive term sheet. It stays out of reference calls, data-room revenue work, technical diligence and the decision itself.
3. **Top-firm data tools point at sourcing, not verification.** Motherbrain, Beacon and a16z's internal tools find *who to talk to*. None of their public descriptions mentions checking deck claims against filings, which is a real gap.
4. **Team claims are what VCs weigh most and check least.** Over 55% of VCs say the team matters most. Team, pedigree, hiring pace, Form D filings and named investors are public before an announcement. ARR, NRR and margins are not.
5. **Tier-1 access is self-reinforcing.** Founders accept 10–14% lower valuations from top VCs (Hsu 2004). With a top VC at seed, the chance of a Series A is 51% vs. a 35% baseline, and drops to 27% if that VC doesn't follow on. Most of their outperformance comes from picking (Sørensen 2007).
6. **Top 5 signals to add:** customer-logo check, hiring from public job boards (Ashby, Greenhouse, Lever), GitHub/npm/PyPI traction, founder history, and hot-deal flags (Form Ds filed by investment vehicles carrying the company's name, plus Tranco traffic trend).
7. **New idea:** use the directors and executives named on the Form D, plus former employees on LinkedIn, as targets for backchannel reference calls.
